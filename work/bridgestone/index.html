Bridgestone FleetFix: five fleet tools, researched into one

The short version

Problem

Tire health data was split across five platforms. Fleet managers had no single view, alerts were overwhelming or missing, and every role saw the same screen.

What I did

Led a team of five through research with fleet managers and technicians, then turned the findings into one role-adaptive platform — testing and rebuilding what didn't work.

Outcome

7.5-second average task time, 75% fewer errors, zero failed usability tests — and first place at the UMSI 2025 Showcase.

Findings → decisions

What users told us, and what changed

Finding 01

The health score felt arbitrary

Users couldn't tell what the number meant. We replaced it with a percentage breakdown by tire status, using consistent red, yellow and green status colors on every screen.

Finding 02

Fleet problems change daily, not monthly

A monthly calendar hid what mattered this week. Weekly became the default, with per-technician scheduling and status at a glance.

Finding 03

Checkboxes weren't a call to action

On tire alerts, users didn't know what checking a box would do. Explicit “Select response” buttons made the next step obvious.

What didn't make it: our health score

It looked great on paper and tested poorly. Instead of defending it, we listened and rebuilt it. Being willing to kill a feature that fails in practice is something I carry into every project.
